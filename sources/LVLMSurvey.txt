Efficient Inference for Large Vision-Language Models: Bottlenecks, Techniques, and Prospects
Jun Zhang, Yicheng Ji, Feiyang Ren, Yihang Li, Bowen Zeng, Zonghao Chen, Ke Chen, Lidan Shou, Gang Chen, Huan Li

Keywords: Large Vision-Language Models, Efficient Inference
Abstract:

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) enable sophisticated reasoning over images and videos, yet their inference is hindered by a systemic efficiency barrier known as visual token dominance. This overhead is driven by a multi-regime interplay between high-resolution feature extraction, quadratic attention scaling, and memory bandwidth constraints. We present a systematic taxonomy of efficiency techniques structured around the inference lifecycle, consisting of encoding, prefilling, and decoding. Unlike prior reviews focused on isolated optimizations, we analyze the end-to-end pipeline to reveal how upstream decisions dictate downstream bottlenecks, covering compute-bound visual encoding, the intensive prefilling of massive contexts, and the ''visual memory wall'' in bandwidth-bound decoding. By decoupling the efficiency landscape into the axes of shaping information density, managing long-context attention, and overcoming memory limits, this work provides a structured analysis of how isolated optimizations compose to navigate the trade-off between visual fidelity and system efficiency. The survey concludes by outlining four future frontiers supported by pilot empirical insights, including hybrid compression based on functional unit sensitivity, modality-aware decoding with relaxed verification, progressive state management for streaming continuity, and stage-disaggregated serving through hardware-algorithm co-design. The submitted software contains a snapshot of our literature repository, which is designed to be maintained as a living resource for the community.

We thank the reviewer for highlighting the importance of the performance-efficiency trade-off. We agree that the viability of lossy compression hinges on its ability to maintain accuracy while reducing resource consumption. While visual token pruning has been extensively discussed in recent literature [1], its impact on the KV cache during the decoding stage remains an emerging bottleneck. To avoid redundancy and provide novel insights, we provide a dedicated analysis in Appendix I: Efficiency-Performance Trade-offs in KV Cache Compression focusing on the varying granularities of KV cache compression. This analysis evaluates how different levels of structural pruning impact the Pareto frontier of MLLMs.

Our empirical findings from Table 1 and Table 2 reveal three key insights into how different compression levels impact the Pareto frontier:

Hierarchy of Compression Granularity: Our analysis compares methods across three distinct dimensions. SnapKV [2] and LOOK-M [3] operate at the token-level by identifying salient visual tokens. MadaKV [4] introduces layer-level adaptability by distributing budgets based on modality preferences across the network depth. Finally, SparseMM [5] operates at the head-level by allocating budgets to specific attention heads. Our results indicate that head-level and layer-level methods generally provide a more robust trade-off than uniform token-level pruning.

Redundancy and Scaling Law Resilience: As shown in Table 1, current MLLMs exhibit significant inherent redundancy. Advanced strategies such as SparseMM and MadaKV show negligible degradation across diverse tasks compared to the Full Cache baseline. Accuracy preservation is notably more pronounced in larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B. This suggests that high-capacity models develop more robust representations that are less sensitive to fine-grained pruning at the head or layer level.

Performance under Extreme Budget Constraints: Table 2 explores the performance limit. While most methods are robust down to a 20% budget, performance diverges sharply at the 5% level. Notably, SparseMM demonstrates superior resilience at ultra-sparse levels compared to token-level methods. This underscores the importance of head-level modality awareness when resources are critically scarce, as it preserves the most semantically relevant attention paths.
